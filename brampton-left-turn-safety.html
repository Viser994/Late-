Local context

Why Brampton intersections need extra focus

Left turns fail most often where speed, size, and signal complexity collide. Brampton’s arterials pack all three into every rush hour.

High-volume multi-lane arterials

Bovaird Drive, Queen Street, Steeles Avenue, and Hurontario/Main carry stacked through-lanes and dedicated turn bays. Gaps close fast—position early and commit only when the path is clear.

Advanced green arrow phases

Many Brampton signals lead with a protected left arrow, then flip to a permissive solid green. Know which phase you are in—an arrow is protected; a circle green is not.

Heavy commercial truck traffic

Distribution corridors and industrial routes mean long vehicles that block sightlines and need wider turning arcs. Wait an extra beat when a truck or large SUV hides opposing lanes.

Defensive sequence

Step-by-step safe left turn guide

Five habits that keep left turns predictable—for you, opposing traffic, and people in the crosswalk.

  1. 1
    Positioning & signaling

    Enter the dedicated left-turn lane early

    Merge into the turn bay as soon as it opens. Signal continuously, square your vehicle in the lane, and avoid last-second lane cuts that surprise following drivers on Queen, Bovaird, or Steeles.

  2. 2
    Waiting safely

    Keep wheels straight while you wait

    If you edge into the intersection on a permissive green, leave your front wheels pointed straight. A rear-end bump with wheels already turned can shove you into oncoming traffic.

  3. 3
    Signals & sightlines

    Read the signal—and look past the blockage

    A green arrow means opposing traffic is held; a solid green circle means you yield. When a truck or large SUV blocks your view, wait until you can see the full opposing lane—not just a sliver of headlights.

  4. 4
    Pedestrians & cyclists

    Scan the crosswalk you are turning into

    Before you accelerate through the turn, check the far-side crosswalk and bike lane. Pedestrians and cyclists have the right-of-way—especially near transit stops and school routes.

  5. 5
    Clearing on amber/red

    Finish the turn if you are already committed

    If you are lawfully in the intersection when the light turns amber or red, complete the turn carefully once the path is clear. Do not freeze mid-box—and do not rush blindly into a gap you have not verified.
